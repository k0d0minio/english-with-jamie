# Voice — how student-facing text sounds

Written for one reader, who is sixteen-ish, sharp, and a little unsure of herself.

- **Warm, direct, adult.** Talk to her the way a good older cousin would. No exclamation
  marks for enthusiasm, no "awesome", no emoji, no "let's learn!".
- **Short.** A slide is a glance, not a page: a heading and up to six bullets or cards,
  each a sentence or two. A reference page is under 600 words. A homework task fits in
  one paragraph.
- **Examples before rules.** Show the sentence first; explain second; keep the
  grammar label last and optional.
- **Real situations, real lines.** Prefer "What you could say at the dinner table on the
  first night" to "Dialogue practice". Give actual phrases she can steal.
- **Say why.** One clause of motivation per task: *…because people will ask you this in
  the first week.*
- **British spelling, Canadian vocabulary** when the two differ. *Colour*, *washroom*.
- **French contrast when it helps**, never as a crutch: *French says "j'ai mangé";
  English splits it in two.*
- **No praise inflation.** "Correct" and "Not quite" are enough; the app adds the rest.

## Markdown conventions

- Headings start at `##` inside a file (the app renders the title from frontmatter).
- Use tables for contrasts (`| French | English |`), bullet lists for phrases, bold for
  the thing to notice, italics for example sentences.
- Pronunciation hints in plain letters: *(sounds like "wen-zday")*, not IPA.
- Links only to things that will still exist in six months.
