English has no future tense. It has several ways of pointing at the future, and the choice tells the listener how sure you are and whether a decision has been made.

## The four you need

**will**: decisions now, predictions, promises, offers. *I'll have the pasta. It'll be cold in January. I'll text you when I land. I'll carry that.*

**going to**: plans already made, predictions from evidence. *I'm going to study in Canada next year. Look at those clouds, it's going to rain.*

**Present continuous**: arrangements with other people. *I'm meeting Anna at six. We're flying on the 28th.*

**Present simple**: timetables. *The train leaves at 9:40. School starts in September.*

## will vs going to

> **A:** We've run out of milk. **B:** Oh, I'**ll** get some. (decided now)
> **A:** Why the coat? **B:** I'**m going to** get some milk. (already decided)

## Softer and more precise

- *I **might** go out later.*
- *I'**m thinking of** doing a gap year.*
- *I'**m about to** leave.*
- *By June I **will have been** there six months.* (future perfect)

## After *when, if, as soon as, before, until*: present, not will

*I'll call you **when I arrive**. If it **rains**, we'll stay in.*
