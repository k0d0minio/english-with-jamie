The passive puts the receiver of the action first and lets you leave out who did it. Form: **be** in the right tense + **past participle**.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| *They clean the rooms daily.* | *The rooms **are cleaned** daily.* |
| *Someone stole my bike.* | *My bike **was stolen**.* |
| *They're building a library.* | *A library **is being built**.* |
| *Nobody has told me.* | *I **haven't been told**.* |
| *They will announce the results.* | *The results **will be announced**.* |
| *You must hand in essays on time.* | *Essays **must be handed in** on time.* |

## When

1. Doer unknown or unimportant: *My phone was stolen.*
2. Doer obvious: *He was arrested.*
3. Formal or objective: *Applications are reviewed within two weeks.*
4. Keep the topic at the front: *The school was founded in 1902 and is still run by the same family.*

Add **by** only when the doer matters: *The novel was written by a teenager.*

## Extras

- **get + participle** (informal): *I got told off. My bag got lost.*
- **have something done**: *I'm having my hair cut on Saturday.*

## Two objects
*They gave me a prize.* → *I **was given** a prize.* (more natural than *A prize was given to me.*)

## Don't overuse it. In conversation, active is the default.
