## Teacher notes
Aim: by the end she can introduce herself, ask three house questions, say what she eats and doesn't, and keep going when she loses a word. Timing: slides 1–2 five minutes, 3–4 fifteen, exercise 1 ten, slides 5–6 ten, exercise 2 ten, final task ten. Prompts to get her talking: "What do you want them to know about you in the first ten minutes?" and "What are you dreading being asked?" Listen for: *I have 15 years*, *I am agree*, present perfect for finished time (*I have arrived yesterday*). Buy-time phrases are the thing to drill; she freezes on open questions.

## Slide: By the end of today
- You can walk in, say who you are, and ask about the house without rehearsing it in French first.
- You can say what you eat and don't eat without apologising for it.
- You have three ways to buy five seconds when a question lands and nothing comes.

## Slide: At the door
What you say in the first thirty seconds matters less than you think. Keep it short and warm.

- *Hi, I'm [your name]. Thank you so much for having me.*
- *It's really nice to finally meet you.*
- *Sorry, I'm a bit wiped from the flight.* (*wiped* = exhausted; very Canadian)
- *Should I take my shoes off?* — yes, always, in Canada. Asking is the polite move.

Notice: **I'm**, not *I am*; **It's**, not *It is*. Contractions are the sound of a relaxed person.

## Slide: Asking about the house
Questions with *could I* and *where do you* are soft and normal. Don't over-apologise; one *sorry* per question is already plenty.

| You want to know | Say |
|---|---|
| where things are | *Where do you keep the glasses?* · *Which bathroom should I use?* |
| what's allowed | *Is it okay if I shower in the morning?* · *Could I use the kitchen if I'm hungry later?* |
| the routine | *What time do you usually have dinner?* · *When do people get up on a school day?* |
| Wi-Fi, obviously | *Could I get the Wi-Fi password?* |

French trap: *the kitchen*, *the dinner*, *the school day* — English drops *the* for routines and meals: *at dinner*, *after school*, *in the morning*.

## Slide: At the table
Food comes up on night one, guaranteed. Have the sentences ready.

- *I eat pretty much everything.* / *I'm not a big fan of…* (softer than *I don't like*)
- *I don't eat meat, but fish is fine.*
- *This is really good — what's in it?*
- *Could I have a bit more?* / *I'm good, thanks.* (= no more, politely)
- *Do you mind if I don't finish? I'm full.*

**I'm good, thanks** is the Canadian *non merci*. It sounds like a yes; it's a no.

## Slide: Buying time
When a question lands and your brain is in French, you need five seconds, not an answer. These sound completely normal.

- *Hmm, good question.*
- *Let me think…*
- *How do I say this…*
- *So, basically…* (then start anywhere)
- *Sorry, what was the word again?*

Say the filler, breathe, then answer in a simple sentence. Nobody is timing you.

## Slide: Saying things about yourself
Short facts, present simple, no *since* trap.

- *I'm from Belgium. I'm in grade ten at home.* (Canada says *grade ten*, not *fourth year*)
- *I've been learning English for about six years.* (present perfect: still true)
- *I knit, and I make bracelets — I'll probably make you one.*
- *I love animals. We have a cat called…* (cat's name, not people's)
- *I'm kind of quiet at first, but I warm up.*

French trap: *since six years* → **for six years**. *Since* takes a point (*since 2020*), *for* takes a length.
