## Teacher notes
Aim: she walks through her first day at an Ottawa high school out loud, and leaves with a two-line introduction, polite questions for anything she needs, and lines to ask for a seat at lunch. This is the first lesson built on a syllabus row, but keep the same shape as the first two: the slides are props, and tangents are welcome. Don't re-teach what *Traditions* covered (Spirit Week, the Terry Fox Run, school colours and mascots, snow days); just point back to it. Her form shaped this lesson: the first day at school was her first pick to practise; meeting classmates for the first time makes her a little nervous (hence the lunch table, the icebreakers and the talk task); she makes things by hand and loves animals (the crafts club, volunteering at a shelter); she's looking forward to winter sports (the curling and ski teams); when she gets stuck she laughs and switches to French (hence *my English is still warming up*); and she asked to be corrected **only on the big things**. Listen for: *Do you know where is…?* (French inverts; English doesn't after *do you know*), *I have 16 years*, *pass a test* meaning *passer* (in English it means she succeeded), *professor* for a school teacher, *a good note* for *a good mark*, *assist to a class* for *attend*, and *I'm here since August* for *I've been here since August*. Sounds: *Ms.* (miz), *th* in *thirty* and *third* when she reads times and floors, and *schedule* (Canadians say "sked-jool"). As written this runs past an hour: skip O Canada, school words, the icebreakers or the 40 hours if talk runs long. Protect the lunch table and the talk task.

## Slide: Today

Next August you'll walk into a Canadian high school where you know nobody. Today we walk through that first day, from the front door to the last bell.

- You'll be able to say who you are and why you're there, in two sentences.
- You'll ask for anything, a room, a rule, a seat at lunch, without sounding blunt.
- You'll read a Canadian timetable and talk about your plans for the semester.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min.
- **Say:** "The first two lessons were the city and the calendar. Today is the building you'll spend most of your time in."
- **Ask:** "On a scale of one to ten, how nervous are you about the first day? We'll ask again at the end."

## Slide: Who you are
@kicker: Lines to steal · Saying who you are
You'll say this twenty times in the first week. Have it ready, so you can think about the person instead of the grammar.

**At the main office**
- *Hi, I'm a new exchange student. I'm supposed to pick up my timetable.*
- *Sorry, I'm looking for the guidance office.*

**When the class asks**
- *I'm from Belgium, the French-speaking part.*
- *I'm staying with a host family here until February.*
- *Back home I'm in 4e secondaire. Here it's about grade 10.*

**When you get stuck**
- *Sorry, my English is still warming up.* Then keep going, in English.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Do:** be the receptionist, then a curious classmate. Ask "Where are you from?" · "How long are you here for?" · "Why Canada?"
- **Adjust the grade line** to whichever grade the school gives her.
- **Listen for:** *I have 16 years* (*I'm 16*), and *I stay* for *I'm staying*.

## Slide: Asking without sounding blunt
@kicker: Lines to steal · The grammar
A straight question is fine with friends. With a stranger in a hallway, wrap it.

| Straight | Softer |
|---|---|
| *Where's the gym?* | *Do you know where the gym **is**?* |
| *What time does lunch start?* | *Could you tell me what time lunch **starts**?* |
| *Is this Room 214?* | *Do you know **if** this is Room 214?* |
| *Who teaches English?* | *Any idea who teaches English?* |

- After *do you know* or *could you tell me*, the question goes back to normal order: **subject, then verb**. No *do* or *does*.
- French flips it, *tu sais où est le gymnase ?*, so the trap is *do you know where is the gym*.
- A yes/no question gets *if* (or *whether*).

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Do:** fire straight questions at her ("Where's the library?") and have her soften each one.
- **Listen for:** *where is the gym* inside the wrapped question. That's the big one today.

## Slide: The main office
@art: secret
@kicker: Part 1 · The first morning
@stat: Day 1
Your first stop isn't a classroom.

- Most schools want a new student at the **main office** before classes start. You sign in, and someone walks you to **guidance**.
- A **guidance counsellor** builds your timetable, helps you choose courses and fixes problems. Probably your first friendly adult in the building.
- You'll usually leave with a printed timetable, a locker number and a student card with your photo.
- Often a student volunteer, a *buddy*, shows you around on the first day.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Ask:** "Who did that job at your school back home?" · "What would you want to ask a guidance counsellor?"
- **Language:** *counsellor* (British spelling, Canadian job), *sign in*, *show someone around*.

## Slide: Reading your timetable
@kicker: Part 1 · The first morning
A typical day at an Ottawa high school. Yours will look something like this.

| Period | Time | Course | Room |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8:30 – 9:45 | English | 214 |
| 2 | 9:50 – 11:05 | Math | 108 |
| Lunch | 11:05 – 11:50 | | Cafeteria |
| 3 | 11:55 – 1:10 | French | Portable 3 |
| 4 | 1:15 – 2:30 | Phys ed | Gym B |

- Many Ontario high schools use **semesters**: four courses from September to January, four new ones from February. Usually the same timetable every day.
- So classes are long, about **75 minutes** each.
- A **portable** is a classroom in a trailer outside the main building. Yes, you walk there in the snow.
- In grades 11 and 12, some students get a **spare**: a period with no class.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Go back to her guess** about class length.
- **Ask:** "What's your first class? When's lunch? Where's French?" She reads times aloud: *eleven-oh-five*, *one-fifteen*.
- **Note:** she arrives for semester one and leaves around the switch in February, so she may see two timetables.
- **Listen for:** *th* in *thirty*; *Math* (Canada) vs *Maths* (UK).

## Slide: Your locker
@art: locks
@kicker: Part 1 · The first morning
@stat: 3 numbers
Lockers are real, and so is the panic of opening one with people waiting behind you.

- Most lockers close with a **combination lock**: a dial and three numbers.
- Turn **right** three full times and stop at the first number. Turn left, past the first number once, and stop at the second. Turn right to the third, and pull.
- Practise at home first. Nobody gets it on the first try.
- *Sorry, I think this one's mine.* · *Do you know how these locks work?*

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min.
- **Do:** have her say the steps back to you with *first, then, once it's…*
- **Language:** *combination*, *dial*, *jammed* (*My locker's jammed.*).

## Slide: O Canada
@art: flag
@kicker: Part 1 · The first morning
@stat: 1880
- In Ontario schools, the day starts with *O Canada* over the speakers, then the **morning announcements**. Everyone stands up.
- The anthem was written in French first, in 1880, for the Saint-Jean-Baptiste celebrations in Quebec. The English words came in 1908.
- It became the official national anthem in 1980.
- In 2018 one line changed: *in all thy sons command* became *in all of us command*, so it's no longer only about sons.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min. Skippable.
- **Ask:** "Do you know the words of the Belgian anthem? Do schools play it?"
- **Say:** "Stand up; nobody expects you to sing. In Ottawa you may hear it in both languages."
- **Language:** *announcements*, *the PA* (the speakers), *stand for the anthem*.

## Slide: Finding your way
@kicker: Lines to steal · Part 2 · In the hallway
The building will be a maze for about three days. Here's how to ask, and what comes back.

**Asking**
- *Sorry, do you know where Room 214 is?*
- *Is this the way to the gym?*
- *I'm a bit lost. Is the cafeteria upstairs?*

**What you'll hear**
- *It's down the hall, on your left.*
- *Go up the stairs, it's the second door.*
- *It's right past the library.*
- *Oh, that's a portable. Go out the back doors.*

*Hall* or *hallway* is the everyday word; *corridor* sounds like a hospital.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Do:** draw a quick floor plan, or use the room she's in. She asks; you answer at normal speed; she points.
- **Then swap:** you're the lost new student, she gives the directions.

## Slide: What fast English sounds like
@kicker: Part 2 · Listen
Teenagers in a hallway don't talk like a textbook. The words melt into each other.

| You'll hear | It means |
|---|---|
| *D'you know where…?* | Do you know where…? |
| *Whaddaya got next?* | What have you got next? |
| *I'm gonna be late.* | I'm going to be late. |
| *You wanna sit with us?* | Do you want to sit with us? |
| *Lemme see your timetable.* | Let me see your timetable. |
| *I dunno.* | I don't know. |

You don't have to talk like this. You do have to understand it.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Do:** cover the right column. Say each line at full speed, twice; she says the full version.
- **Then:** make up three new ones at speed (*Whaddaya think?*, *Wanna come?*, *Gonna rain*). This is the listening thread for today.

## Slide: School words
@layout: cards
@kicker: Part 2 · In the hallway
- **Grade 10.** The year you're in. *What grade are you in?* High school runs from grade 9 to grade 12.
- **Marks.** What you get on a test: *I got a good mark.* Not *a good note*.
- **Guidance.** The office for timetables, courses and problems.
- **Washroom.** The toilets, in Canada. *Bathroom* works too.
- **Hall pass.** Permission to be out of class. Some teachers make you sign out.
- **Phys ed.** P.E., or just *gym*: *I've got gym after lunch.*

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min. Skippable.
- **Ask:** "Which of these is different from what you'd say in French?" (*note*, *gym*, *grade*).
- **Note:** *May I go to the washroom?* is the classic in-class question; the full lesson on class is later.

## Slide: Can I sit here?
@kicker: Lines to steal · Part 3 · At lunch
The lunch table is where most friendships start, and where most first days feel longest.

**Asking**
- *Is anyone sitting here?*
- *Mind if I join you?*
- *Hi, I'm new. Is this seat taken?*
- *What's good in the cafeteria?*
- *Is there a line for the microwave?* (a *line*, in Canada; *queue* sounds British)

**What you'll hear**
- *Go for it.* · *Yeah, sure, sit down.*
- *Sorry, it's saved.* Someone's coming back. It's not about you; try the next seat.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Do:** be a table of three. Let her ask, then chat at normal speed; she has to ask one follow-up question.
- **This is the moment** her form says she's nervous about. Run it twice: once friendly, once with *Sorry, it's saved* first.

## Slide: Sabrina's Law
@art: cow
@kicker: Part 3 · At lunch
@stat: 2005
- In 2003, Sabrina Shannon, a 13-year-old from Pembroke, Ontario, about an hour and a half from Ottawa, bought fries in her school cafeteria.
- She had serious food allergies, including to milk. The fries had probably touched dairy, and she had a severe reaction and died.
- Her mother campaigned, and in 2005 Ontario passed *Sabrina's Law*, the first law of its kind in the world: every school must have a plan for students with severe allergies.
- That's why many schools ask you not to bring nuts, and why teachers know who carries an *EpiPen*. Before you pack Nutella, ask.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min. Calmer tone.
- **Ask:** "Is anyone at your school allergic to something? What do they do?"
- **Practical:** check the school's rules before her first packed lunch; *Is this nut-free?* is a normal question to ask anyone.
- **Language:** *allergic to*, *nut-free*, *a severe reaction*.

## Slide: Mr, Ms or Miss?
@kicker: Part 3 · Listen and say
Teachers are *Mr.* or *Ms.* plus their surname. Never a first name, never just *Teacher*.

| You see | You say | For |
|---|---|---|
| Mr. Tremblay | *Mister* | a man |
| Ms. Singh | *Miz* (sounds like "miz") | any woman: the safe choice |
| Mrs. Brown | *Missiz* | a married woman who prefers it |
| Miss | *Miss* | what students say to get any woman teacher's attention: *Miss, can I ask something?* |

- In French class it's *Madame* or *Monsieur*, like home.
- Don't call a teacher *professor*: that's university only.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min.
- **Pronunciation:** *Ms.* is a buzzing *z*, not *miss*. Drill *Ms. Singh*, *Mrs. Brown*, *Mr. Tremblay*.
- **Listen for:** *professor* for any teacher; it'll come up.

## Slide: Icebreakers
@layout: cards
@art: camera
@kicker: Part 3 · New people
The first class of a new semester often starts with a game, so the class learns your name.

- **Two truths and a lie.** Say three things about yourself; everyone guesses which one is false.
- **Find someone who…** Walk around and find a classmate who has a dog, who has been to Europe, who plays hockey.
- **Would you rather…** *Would you rather never see snow again, or never see summer again?*
- **This or that.** Poutine or pizza? Cats or dogs? Summer or winter?

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min. Skippable.
- **Do:** play one round of two truths and a lie each, you first.
- **Then say:** "Pick your three for the first day now. You'll use them at the end."

## Slide: Clubs Day
@layout: cards
@art: lakes
@kicker: Part 4 · Your semester
In September, clubs set up tables in the gym or the cafeteria. Joining one is the fastest way to friends outside your classes.

- **Crafts club.** Knitting, sewing, making things while you talk. Lots of schools have one.
- **Outdoor club.** Hiking and canoeing in the autumn, snowshoeing in winter.
- **Curling or ski team.** Try-outs in the autumn. You already know about sweeping.
- **Improv.** Make up scenes on the spot. Terrifying, then great for your English.
- **Yearbook.** Take the photos for the book everyone signs in June.
- **Eco club.** Gardens, recycling, sometimes animal projects.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 3 min.
- **Ask:** "Which table do you walk to first?" She makes things by hand and likes winter sports: the crafts club and the curling team are easy wins.
- **Line to steal:** *How do I sign up?* · *When do you meet?*
- **Language:** *sign up*, *try out* (*try-outs*), *on the spot*.

## Slide: 40 hours
@art: tree
@kicker: Part 4 · Your semester
@stat: 40 hours
- To graduate from an Ontario high school, every student needs **40 hours of community involvement**: unpaid volunteering.
- So your classmates are always looking for hours: food banks, coaching kids' sports, helping at events, walking dogs at an animal shelter.
- Going with them is a shortcut to friends, and to a lot of real Canadian English.
- *Are you doing any volunteering? Could I come along?*

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min. Skippable.
- **She loves animals:** shelters often take teenage volunteers, though age rules vary. A good question for the host family.
- **Ask:** "Do Belgian schools ask for anything like this?"
- **Language:** *volunteer* (noun and verb), *come along*, *graduate*.

## Slide: Marks
@art: trophy
@kicker: Part 4 · Your semester
@stat: 50%
- High school report cards in Ontario give a **percentage** for each course. 50% is a pass.
- Sounds familiar: back home, 10 out of 20 is usually a pass too.
- Canadians say *marks*: *I got 85 on the test.* But *grade* has two jobs: your mark, or the year you're in. Context tells you which.
- **Careful:** *I passed the test* means you succeeded. For the French *passer un examen*, say *I'm writing a test tomorrow*, or *taking* one.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min.
- **Ask:** "What's your best subject? What would you be happy getting over there?"
- **Listen for:** *pass a test* for *passer*. This one causes real confusion: a classmate will think she's sure she'll succeed.

## Slide: Plans with *going to*
@kicker: Lines to steal · The grammar
When you've already decided, English uses *going to*. French does the same with *je vais*, which makes this one easy.

- *I'm going to join the outdoor club.*
- *What are you going to take next semester?*
- *I'm going to try out for the curling team.*
- *Are you going to the game on Friday?* (here *going to* is just a place)

- In fast speech it's *gonna*. Hear it; say it if you like; don't write it.
- For something you decide on the spot, use *will*: *Oh, I'll come with you.*

### Teacher
- **Time:** 2 min.
- **Ask:** "Tell me three things you're going to do in your first month." Get full sentences.
- **Listen for:** *I go to join* (missing *going to*), and *will* for a plan she made weeks ago.

## Slide: Now talk
@layout: talk
@art: chat
It's your first morning. Jamie is your guidance counsellor. Introduce yourself, explain why you're here, and ask three things you need to know: where something is, when something happens, and how something works. Ask them the soft way.

Then the bell goes for lunch, and Jamie is a classmate at a full table. Ask to sit down, play two truths and a lie, and tell him what you're going to do this semester. Stay in English, even when you get stuck.

### Teacher
- **Time:** 8 min.
- **Round one:** be a busy but kind counsellor. Answer her questions briefly and at speed, so she has to ask a follow-up (*Sorry, could you say that again?*).
- **Round two:** this is the meeting she said makes her a little nervous. Start with *Sorry, it's saved*, then let her in at the next seat. React big to her two truths and a lie. Ask *What are you gonna do this semester?* at full speed.
- **If she stalls,** hand her *Sorry, my English is still warming up* rather than letting her switch to French.
- **Close:** ask the one-to-ten nervous question from the start again. One or two big mistakes only, as she asked; one specific thing she did well.
