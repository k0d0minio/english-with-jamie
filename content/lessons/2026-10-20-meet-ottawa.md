## Teacher notes
Aim: she enjoys an hour of English about where she's going, talks more than you do, and leaves curious about Ottawa. No drilling today; the slides are props, and a good tangent beats the next slide. Timing: slides 1–2 ten minutes (let the warm-up run if it's going well), slide 3 five, exercise 1 and the answers fifteen, slide 5 five, exercise 2 and the stories fifteen, final task ten. Prompts to get her talking: "What's the first thing you want to eat over there?" and "Which of these would you tell your friends at home about first?" Nudges if the warm-up stalls: Céline Dion, Drake, Ryan Reynolds, *Schitt's Creek*, maple syrup, moose. Listen for: articles on place names (*the Canada*, *in the Ottawa*), *I have been there last year* (past simple for finished time), *actually* meaning *currently*, and the stress on Otta-WA. Don't correct mid-flow; jot things down and pick two at the end. Spare stories if she wants more: the Library of Parliament survived the 1916 fire because a clerk shut its iron doors; *Maman*, the nine-metre bronze spider outside the National Gallery; the Diefenbunker, a Cold War nuclear bunker four storeys underground, now a museum; and *In Flanders Fields*, the poem Canadians read every Remembrance Day (11 November), written by a Canadian doctor near Ypres in 1915 — Belgium is already part of Canada's story.

## Slide: Today
No grammar test. This is an hour of talking about the place you're going to live.

- By the end, you'll know a few things about Ottawa that most people who live there don't.
- You'll have a handful of ways to guess and react in English, so a surprising fact never leaves you silent.
- And you'll have talked for an hour without thinking about it too much.

## Slide: What you already know
No wrong answers. Just talk.

- Three words that come to mind when you hear *Canada*.
- Anything Canadian you've watched, eaten or listened to.
- What you picture when you think of Ottawa. "Nothing" is an honest answer.
- The Canadian animal you'd most like to see in real life.
- One thing you're curious about, or slightly nervous about.

## Slide: Guessing and reacting
Two quizzes are coming. Here's how people sound when they're not sure, and when they're surprised.

| You're guessing | It's surprising | It makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| *My guess is…* | *No way.* | *That makes sense.* |
| *I'd say about…* | *Wait, really?* | *Oh, I've heard of that.* |
| *No idea, but I'm going to say…* | *That's wild.* | *That's so Canadian.* |
| *It's either… or…* | *I had no idea.* | *I'd love to see that.* |

**I'd** is *I would*: the soft way to guess or to want. French says *je dirais*; English says *I'd say forty million*.

## Slide: Canada, the answers
- **Just over 40 million people**, in the second-biggest country on Earth. Most of them live fairly close to the US border.
- **More lakes than the rest of the world put together.**
- Letters to Santa go to **H0H 0H0**, and Canada Post volunteers write back.
- The Great Canadian **Maple Syrup** Heist: about 18 million dollars of syrup, stolen from a warehouse in Quebec. Quebec makes most of the world's supply.
- **Hawaiian pizza** was invented in Ontario in 1962, by a Greek-Canadian restaurant owner. People still argue about it.
- A **loonie** is the $1 coin; it has a loon, a water bird, on it. The $2 coin is a **toonie**.

## Slide: Why Ottawa?
- In 1857 Queen Victoria had to pick a capital. Toronto, Kingston, Montreal and Quebec City all wanted it.
- She chose a rough lumber town between them: right on the line between English-speaking Ontario and French-speaking Quebec, and harder for an American army to reach.
- Its old name was **Bytown**. *Ottawa* comes from an Algonquin word meaning *to trade*.
- Cross a bridge over the river and you're in **Gatineau, Quebec**, where French is the language of the street. Your French will come in handy.
- Say it like a local: **OT-ta-wa**, stress on the first part, and the middle *t* goes soft *(sounds like "AH-duh-wuh")*. French puts the stress at the end, and people will hear it straight away.

## Slide: Ottawa's strangest stories
- **The canal.** The Rideau Canal was finished in 1832 in case of war with the Americans. Now, every winter, 7.8 km of it freezes into the world's biggest natural skating rink, and people skate to work.
- **The princess.** In the Second World War, part of the Dutch royal family lived in Ottawa. When Princess Margriet was born in 1943, her hospital room was declared outside Canada so she would be born only Dutch. The Netherlands still sends tulips every year to say thank you.
- **The cats.** For decades, stray cats lived on Parliament Hill in little wooden houses built by volunteers. The last ones were adopted in 2013.
- **The pastry.** A *BeaverTail* is fried dough stretched flat like a beaver's tail, with cinnamon sugar on top. When Obama visited in 2009, the stand renamed it the *Obamatail*.
- **The cold.** January sits around −10°C, and the wind can make it feel like −30. One of the coldest capitals in the world, but Ulaanbaatar, in Mongolia, is colder.
