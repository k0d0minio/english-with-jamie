These answers decide what we talk about from lesson two onwards. There are no right
answers and nothing is marked. Write in English, but don't polish it: a quick, honest
answer is more useful than a perfect one. Skip anything you like, save it and come back,
and send it when you're happy.

## Teacher notes
- **Purpose:** find the material for lessons 2–14: what she loves, which Ottawa moments
  she wants to rehearse, what worries her, and how she wants to be taught.
- **Things you love** → examples, stories and talk tasks in every lesson; seeds for the
  watch and read lists.
- **Ottawa** → which syllabus rows to bring forward; the "dread" answer is a lesson of
  its own.
- **Speaking English** → the scales are a baseline to ask again in December; the "got
  stuck" story is a ready-made role-play.
- **Our lessons** → correction style and homework load; follow it from the next lesson.

## Things you love

```questions
long: What does a perfect free Saturday look like for you? || Morning to evening, as much or as little as you like.
text: What are you making at the moment, or what did you make last? || Knitting, drawing, baking, anything.
long: Tell me about an animal in your life, now or ever. || A pet, a neighbour's dog, one you'd love to have.
text: A series or film you'd happily watch again
text: A song or artist you have on repeat right now
many: Which of these would you enjoy a lesson about? | Animals and wildlife | Making things by hand | Music | Series and films | The outdoors and winter sports | Food and cooking | Something else…
```

## Ottawa

Six months is a long time. Here is where we decide which moments to practise first.

```questions
long: What are you most looking forward to? || The thing you'd tell a friend about.
many: Which of these would you like to feel ready for? | The first dinner with the host family | The first day at school | Making friends | Asking a teacher for help | Shops, cafés and ordering food | Buses, the O-Train and getting lost | Phone calls and appointments | Winter | Something else…
one: Which one would you most like to practise first? | The first dinner with the host family | The first day at school | Making friends | Asking a teacher for help | Shops, cafés and ordering food | Buses, the O-Train and getting lost | Phone calls and appointments | Winter
long: Is there a moment you're a little nervous about? || Only if you want to say. Saying it out loud is the first step to practising it.
```

## Speaking English

```questions
scale: Speaking English out loud | I freeze | I'm at ease
scale: Understanding people who speak fast | I lose it quickly | I follow everything
long: Tell me about a time you got stuck speaking English. What happened? || Where you were, what you wanted to say, what came out.
text: A word or phrase you always mix up or can never remember
one: When you watch something in English, what's on screen? | No subtitles | English subtitles | French subtitles | It depends
```

## Our lessons

```questions
one: When you make a mistake, how would you like me to correct you? | Straight away | At the end of the activity | Only the big ones | I don't mind
one: How much homework feels right each week? | Ten minutes | Twenty minutes | Half an hour or more
many: What helps you learn? | Talking about something real | Games and quizzes | Clear rules and tables | Videos and songs | Reading | Something else…
long: By Christmas, what would you like to be able to say or do in English that you can't yet?
long: Anything else I should know, or anything you'd rather we didn't talk about?
```
