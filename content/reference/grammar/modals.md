Modals are the small verbs before the main verb: *can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would*. They never change form and take a bare infinitive (*must go*, not ~~must to go~~).

## Ability
*can / can't* (now), *could / couldn't* (past), *be able to* (other tenses). *I could swim at five. I wasn't able to finish.*

## Permission and requests
*Can I…? Could I…? May I…?* in rising politeness. *Could you open the window? Would you mind opening it?*

## Obligation
- *must*: the speaker's own rule. *I must call my mum.*
- *have to*: external rule. *We have to wear uniform.*
- *mustn't*: prohibited. *You mustn't tell anyone.*
- *don't have to*: not necessary. *You don't have to come.* (very different from *mustn't*)
- *should / ought to*: advice. *You should get some sleep.*

## Deduction

| Sure it's true | Possible | Sure it's false |
|---|---|---|
| *must* | *may / might / could* | *can't* |

*She must be tired. He might be on the bus. That can't be Tom, he's in Spain.*

## Past: modal + have + participle
*You must have left it at school. They might have missed the train. I should have studied more. You needn't have bought bread.*

## Softening
*I was wondering if you could help me. Would it be OK if I left early? I might just take one, if that's all right.*

## Practice
One piece of advice, one deduction about where a friend is right now, one regret about last week.
