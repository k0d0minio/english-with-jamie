The slip that marks a French speaker fastest: *j'ai mangé* looks like *I have eaten* but usually means *I ate*.

## One line

**Past simple** = finished time. **Present perfect** = time not finished, or the result matters now.

## Finished time → past simple

- *I **saw** her yesterday.*
- *We **moved** here in 2021.*
- ***Did** you **enjoy** the film?*

Words that force it: *yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago, when I was ten, at the weekend.*

## Unfinished time or present result → present perfect

- *I**'ve seen** her today.* (today isn't over)
- *We**'ve lived** here since 2021.* (still do)
- *I**'ve lost** my phone.* (don't have it now)
- ***Have** you ever **been** to Canada?* (in your life so far)

Words that go with it: *today, this week, since, for, ever, never, already, yet, just, recently, so far.*

## The switch

> **A:** Have you ever been to London?
> **B:** Yes, I have. I **went** last spring.
> **A:** What **did** you **do** there?

"In your life so far" → present perfect. A specific finished trip → past simple. Native speakers switch within one breath. Copy that.

## North America

People often use past simple where British English uses present perfect: *Did you eat yet? I already did it.* Both are fine; understand both.

## Practice

1. I ___ (never / try) poutine. → *have never tried*
2. We ___ (arrive) an hour ago. → *arrived*
3. She ___ (not / finish) yet. → *hasn't finished*
4. When ___ you ___ (start) English? → *did … start*
