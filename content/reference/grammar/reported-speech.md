When you tell someone what another person said, the verb usually moves one step into the past: backshift.

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"I **am** tired."* | *She said she **was** tired.* |
| *"I **work** Saturdays."* | *He said he **worked** Saturdays.* |
| *"I **have finished**."* | *She said she **had finished**.* |
| *"I **went** home."* | *He said he **had gone** home.* |
| *"I **will** call."* | *She said she **would** call.* |
| *"I **can** help."* | *He said he **could** help.* |
| *"I **must** go."* | *She said she **had to** go.* |

## Other changes
- Pronouns: *"I like your idea"* → *She said she liked my idea.*
- Time and place: *today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before, here → there.*

## No backshift
If it's still true or reported immediately: *She said she **lives** in Ottawa. He just told me the bus **leaves** at six.*

## Questions
Normal word order, no question mark. *"Where do you live?"* → *He asked where I **lived**.* *"Are you OK?"* → *She asked **if** I was OK.*

## Requests and advice
*"Please wait."* → *She asked me **to wait**.* *"Don't touch that."* → *He told me **not to touch** it.* *"You should rest."* → *She advised me **to rest**.*

## Better than *said*
*explained, admitted, promised, suggested, warned, complained, insisted, denied, pointed out.* *She suggested going out. He promised to be on time.*

## Practice
Three things a friend said to you this week, without using *said*.
