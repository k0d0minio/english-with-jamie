A story needs three past forms working together: a timeline, some background, and the occasional flashback.

| Form | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past simple** | main events, in order | *I **opened** the door and **saw** him.* |
| **Past continuous** | background, already happening | *It **was raining** and everyone **was looking** at their phones.* |
| **Past perfect** | a step further back | *He **had left** his wallet at home.* |

## Together

> It **was** late and the streets **were** empty. I **was walking** home when I **heard** footsteps. I **turned** round. Nobody. Then I **realised** I **had forgotten** my keys.

## When past perfect is not needed

If *before*, *after* or *then* already gives the order, past simple is fine: *After I **finished** school, I **moved** to Ottawa.* Use *had* when the order would otherwise be unclear, or when the earlier event explains the later one.

## Past perfect continuous

How long something had been going on before the main event: *She **had been waiting** for an hour when the bus came.*

## Story language

- Scene: *It was a Tuesday afternoon… I was sixteen at the time…*
- Sequence: *then, after that, suddenly, meanwhile, in the end, eventually*
- Reaction: *I couldn't believe it. I had no idea what to do.*

## Practice

Tell the story of your last journey in six sentences: two background, three events, one flashback.
