Questions are where politeness lives. Three skills: forming them fast, adding tags, and making them indirect.

## Direct questions
Helper verb before the subject: *Do you like it? Where did she go? Have you finished? Can I sit here?* Subject questions keep normal order: *Who called? What happened?*

## Question tags
Statement + opposite mini-question. Positive → negative tag; negative → positive.
- *You're from Belgium, **aren't you**?*
- *She doesn't eat meat, **does she**?*
- *We've met before, **haven't we**?*
- *Let's go, **shall we**?* / *Pass the salt, **will you**?* / *I'm late, **aren't I**?*

Falling intonation = you're sure (just checking). Rising = real question.

## Indirect questions: the polite ones
Start with a frame, then normal word order, no *do*.
- *Where is the station?* → *Could you tell me **where the station is**?*
- *Does this bus go downtown?* → *Do you know **if this bus goes** downtown?*
- *What time does it start?* → *I was wondering **what time it starts**.*

Frames: *Could you tell me… / Do you know… / I'd like to know… / Any idea…*

## Echo and reaction questions
*You're moving to Canada? For six months? Really?* Natural, friendly, and great for keeping a conversation going.

## Practice
Ask a stranger for three things (a direction, the time, a seat) using indirect questions.
