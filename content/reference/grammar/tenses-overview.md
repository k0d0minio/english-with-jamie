English has two real tenses, present and past, and builds the rest with helper verbs: **be**, **have**, **will**. Hold on to that and the "twelve tenses" become one grid.

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | I work | I am working | I have worked | I have been working |
| **Past** | I worked | I was working | I had worked | I had been working |
| **Future** | I will work | I will be working | I will have worked | I will have been working |

## What the columns mean

- **Simple**: a fact, a habit, a finished event. *I live in Brussels. She called yesterday.*
- **Continuous**: in progress, temporary, unfinished at a moment. *I'm living with a host family this year.*
- **Perfect**: looking back from a point in time; the result matters. *I've lost my keys* (so I can't get in).
- **Perfect continuous**: how long something has been going on. *I've been waiting for forty minutes.*

## The ones you really use

In order of frequency at B2: present simple, past simple, present perfect, present continuous, *going to* / *will*, past continuous. Past perfect and the perfect continuous forms are for stories and complaints. Future perfect forms: recognise them, don't force them.

## Self-check

Say each pair out loud. Finished or not? Habit or right now? Result now or just history?

1. I've eaten. / I ate at six.
2. She's working in Ottawa. / She works in Ottawa.
3. They'd left when we arrived. / They left when we arrived.
