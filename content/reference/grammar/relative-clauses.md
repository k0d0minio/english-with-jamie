Relative clauses glue extra information onto a noun. The pronoun and the commas change the meaning.

| People | Things | Possession | Place | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| who / that | which / that | whose | where | when |

## Defining (no commas): which one?
*The girl **who** sits next to me is from Ottawa.* Without the clause you don't know which girl. *That* is fine here, and the pronoun can disappear when it's the object: *The book (that) I lent you.*

## Non-defining (commas): extra information
*My host mother, **who** is a nurse, works nights.* You already know who; the clause adds a detail. No *that*, and the pronoun never disappears.

## The commas matter
- *My brother who lives in Paris is a chef.* (I have several brothers)
- *My brother, who lives in Paris, is a chef.* (I have one brother)

## whose, where, when
*The friend **whose** family I'm staying with. The town **where** I'll go to school. The day **when** we arrived.*

## Prepositions
Casual: *the family I'm staying **with**.* Formal: *the family **with whom** I'm staying.* Use the casual one when speaking.

## Reduced clauses
*The students **standing** by the door* (= who are standing). *The essay **written** by Sam* (= which was written).

## Practice
Describe three people in your life using one defining and two non-defining clauses.
